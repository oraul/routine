## Why

`evidence/retro.txt` is committed as "a verbatim render of
`bin/routine-retro` over this machine's runs/". At `v0.14.0` it is not
one: it reports `gate.developer runs=4 fails=2` from 2026-08-14 while
the corpus reports `runs=28 fails=3` — 47 lines of disagreement once
the generator's own timestamp line is set aside. The same stale render
shipped in v0.12.0, v0.13.0 and v0.14.0, recorded as a Gate entry each
time and fixed by nobody.

A committed file that describes itself as a verbatim render, and is
not, is a false claim inside a published artifact. Three releases of
naming it in prose is the evidence that prose does not fix it.

The moment staleness enters is a commit made on a machine that has the
corpus; the moment it becomes public is a release cut from that same
machine. That is where the gate belongs.

## What Changes

- `bin/routine-render-check [file...]` refuses a committed render that
  disagrees with what its own declared generator produces now, naming
  the render and the command that refreshes it.
- Renders identify themselves: the check finds them by their
  `# generated by <command> at <timestamp>` header rather than by a
  hardcoded filename, so a render added later is covered by existing.
- `bin/routine-evidence` declares the corpus its snapshot derives from
  in the header, so a check can tell a stale render from one it has no
  corpus to judge.
- `bin/routine-release-check` invokes the new check and relays its
  verdict, the way it already relays `routine-record-lint`.
- The `evidence` capability's "freshness SHALL NOT be claimed
  mechanically" is amended: it cannot be claimed where the corpus is
  absent, and SHALL be decided where it is present.
- `evidence/retro.txt` is regenerated — the defect the gate exists to
  catch, fixed in the change that catches it.

## What is deliberately not built

- No auto-regeneration inside the gate. A gate that repairs what it
  judges has judged nothing.
- No verdict where the corpus is absent. `runs/` is gitignored, so a
  clean checkout can regenerate nothing; the check says so on stdout
  and decides nothing, rather than passing silently or refusing a
  release it cannot judge.
- No freshness claim for renders committed on another machine. Run
  evidence is machine-local, so a render legitimately differs
  elsewhere; regenerating before cutting a release is the instruction
  that resolves it, and multi-machine releases are the earning
  condition for revisiting.
- No judgment of a record's truth. The check decides renders, which
  are mechanical; whether a lesson is real stays the author's, exactly
  as the record rule already states.
