# Design — a-prediction-names-what-settles-it

## The rule is one sentence, and the sentence does the work

Drafts rejected: "be careful with predictions" (unactionable),
"verify before relaying" (collapses prediction into measurement — a
forecast cannot be verified yet, only labelled). The kept form:

> A claim is either measured — say what ran — or a prediction — name
> the evidence that settles it. Never a third thing.

"Never a third thing" is the load-bearing clause: the failure mode is
precisely the unlabelled middle, a confident sentence that is neither.

## Where it lands in each file

`CLAUDE.md`: beside the evidence rules, stated for the session that
drives — because three of the four exhibits were the driver's, not an
agent's. `agents/analyst.md`: in the output rules, tied to the graders
that already exist, so the instruction adds a label rather than a new
artifact. No new section, no taxonomy, no template — the Nit/Refinement
lesson stands: two classes, one clause.

## Pins

`grep -qi` on words absent today from both files — verified before
this design was written, not assumed: `forecast`, `settles`,
`prediction`, `predicted` all at zero in both. Pin words chosen from
that set; deleting the rule reddens the pin, and no existing sentence
satisfies it.

## Non-goals

- Teaching the skill or developer the rule: the skill's driver IS the
  session CLAUDE.md binds; the developer's claims are script-graded
  within minutes.
- Any enforcement beyond the content pin. The rule is contract; the
  exhibits show the cost of breaking it, and the release record is
  where breaking it gets recorded.
